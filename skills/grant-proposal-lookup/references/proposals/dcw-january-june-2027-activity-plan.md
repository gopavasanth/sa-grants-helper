6. What is the change you are trying to bring? What are the main challenges or problems you are trying to solve? Describe this change or challenges, as well as main approaches to achieve it. (required)

We aim to bridge systemic knowledge gaps surrounding global Muslim academia, history, culture, and regional heritage across Wikimedia projects. Through our network of campus Wiki Clubs, we empower students, researchers, and scholars from minority educational institutions and madrasas to transition from passive consumers of information into active knowledge creators and movement leaders. The core challenges we are addressing include severe underrepresentation of regional academic and cultural topics on Wikimedia projects such as Wikipedia, Wikidata, and Wikimedia Commons; high post-workshop drop-off rates among new contributors; and the lack of custom open-source tools to handle multi-tenant credentialing (issuing verifiable credentials to volunteers for retention), member onboarding, and volunteer support without relying on proprietary SaaS platforms or having emails lost in individual inboxes.

To achieve this change in the January-June 2027 term, we will use several proven, strategic approaches. First, we leverage our successful residential leadership retreat model, Train-a-Wikipedian (TaW), organising one cohort this term to train approximately 15 new and semi-experienced Wikimedians in core Wikimedia policies, guidelines, and projects. We will award Heritage Lens fellowships to document historical monuments and cultural sites on Wikimedia Commons. Additionally, we plan to establish targeted strategic partnerships with WikiProject Med to expand content on Muslims in medicine, collaborate with KHOJ to train artists and researchers to archive creative practices on Commons, and integrate our DCW Certification Portal with Capacity Exchange (CapX) for cross-community credential verification and showcasing through Meta-Wiki. We drive campus outreach through Wiki Clicks AMU and Wiki Clicks Jamia, targeting around 400 media uploads through our clubs, Wiki Club AMU and Wiki Club Jamia. We will also host a hands-on DCW Technical Hackathon supported by mentors from the Indic MediaWiki Developers User Group to upgrade custom open-source tools (dcw-engage and dcw-certificate-portal) while triaging approximately 30 prioritised Phabricator tasks related to Wikimedia extensions and tools. We also implement a structured contributor retention policy through our Mehfil-e-Wiki meetups and the DCW Engage portal, capping volunteer support packages at approximately 80 monthly edits along with mandatory attendance in at least three consecutive Conversation Hours. We also move to awarding redeemable points based on activities, gamifying the participation practices for youth in our community.

9. Who are the target participants and from which community? How will you engage participants before and during the activities? How will you follow up with participants after the activities? (required)

Our target participants include students, researchers, academic scholars, youth, and photography enthusiasts from minority educational institutions and madrasas, including Aligarh Muslim University, Jamia Millia Islamia, and Darul Uloom Deoband, alongside visual artists through KHOJ and medical contributors via WikiProject Med. 17. What do you hope to learn from your work in this project or proposal? (required)

Grounded in our core principle of Evaluate, Iterate, and Adapt, we seek to answer four key learning questions through this six-month implementation. First, we aim to learn whether capping volunteer support at roughly 80 monthly edits alongside mandatory attendance in three consecutive Conversation Hours leads to sustained monthly editing habits rather than short-lived edit spikes. Second, we want to evaluate how effectively integrating the DCW Certification Portal with Capacity Exchange (CapX) facilitates adoption by other Wikimedia user groups and enables digital badge displays on Meta-Wiki user profiles. Third, we seek to determine whether domain-specific collaborations with partners like WikiProject Med and KHOJ yield higher content retention and media quality than generalised campus outreach. Fourth, we aim to assess whether pairing local developers with mentors from the Indic MediaWiki Developers User Group during our technical hackathon expands our long-term technical maintainer pool for dcw-engage and contributes to Wikimedia technical spaces.
