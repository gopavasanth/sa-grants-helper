6. What is the change you are trying to bring? What are the main challenges or problems you are trying to solve? Describe this change or challenges, as well as main approaches to achieve it. (required)

While Phase I [1] (https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Community_Resources_and_Partnerships/India_Rapid_Project/50K_Malayalam_Words:A_Lingua_Libre_Audio_Corpus_Project) laid the bedrock for open-source Malayalam speech datasets by recording isolated lexical headwords on Lingua Libre, isolated word recordings only address a subset of computational and pedagogical needs. Natural language thrives on rhythm, sandhi rules (സ്വരസന്ധി/വ്യഞ്ജനസന്ധി), prosody, tone, and contextual elision—phenomena that disappear when words are pronounced in isolation.

In Phase II, we aim to transition the Malayalam open audio ecosystem from isolated lexical tokens to a comprehensive acoustic archive combining broad lexical scale with continuous, contextual speech. By pairing an additional 50,000 unique lexical recordings with curated, long-form literary and reference sentences from Malayalam Wikisource, this project bridges the critical gap between basic dictionary pronunciation and high-level Natural Language Processing (NLP), Speech-to-Text (STT), and Text-to-Speech (TTS) models.

#### Main Challenges & Problems We Are Trying to Solve

Section link: https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Community_Resources_and_Partnerships/India_Rapid_Project/Malayalam_Audio_Corpus_Phase_II:50K_Words_%26_Wikisource_Audio_Project#Main_Challenges_&_Problems_We_Are_Trying_to_Solve

1. Acoustic Deficiency in Connected Speech: Isolated word models fail to train modern continuous Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) engines. Phonemes in Malayalam shift predictably during connected discourse (e.g., retroflex transitions, gemination, agglutinative inflections). Without aligned continuous sentence data, open acoustic models cannot achieve conversational accuracy
2. Text-Audio Asymmetry on Malayalam Wikimedia Projects: Malayalam Wikisource hosts hundreds of historical, literary, and public-domain texts that remain purely textual. Visually impaired readers, auditory learners, and non-native speakers have limited accessibility options.
3. Recording Complexity of Long-Form Literary Malayalam: Recording formal, classical, and compound-dense prose from Wikisource introduces fatigue, pronunciation variance, breath artifacts, and alignment challenges that do not exist during rapid-fire single-word list capture on Lingua Libre.

#### Main Approaches to Achieve This

Section link: https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Community_Resources_and_Partnerships/India_Rapid_Project/Malayalam_Audio_Corpus_Phase_II:50K_Words_%26_Wikisource_Audio_Project#Main_Approaches_to_Achieve_This

#### 1. Dual-Track Lexical and Sentence Curation

Section link: https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Community_Resources_and_Partnerships/India_Rapid_Project/Malayalam_Audio_Corpus_Phase_II:50K_Words_%26_Wikisource_Audio_Project#1._Dual-Track_Lexical_and_Sentence_Curation

- Track A (50K Lexical Additions): Curate and batch-record an additional 50,000 distinct headwords—targeting morphological inflections, technical terms, and regional colloquialisms missing from Phase I—using established Lingua Libre workflows.
- Track B (Spoken Wikisource Prose &amp; Literary Heritage): Select and record complete, high-value prose works from Malayalam Wikisource (e.g., chapters and legends from Kottarathil Sankunni’s Aithihyamala) as high-fidelity spoken-audio editions. These recordings will be uploaded directly to Wikimedia Commons and embedded into their respective Wikisource pages, converting canonical public-domain prose into accessible, continuous-speech audiobooks while simultaneously generating long-form, paragraph-level audio corpora for speech technologies. Aithihyamala has an average of 1800 words per chapter. Around 50 hours of reading

2. Structured Wikimedia Ingestion & Interoperability

- Upload all recordings to Wikimedia Commons under open licenses with rich, machine-readable metadata.
- Embed continuous speech recordings directly into their corresponding Wikisource pages, enabling read-along accessibility and providing aligned datasets ready for training open speech architectures

9. Who are the target participants and from which community? How will you engage participants before and during the activities? How will you follow up with participants after the activities? (required)

The target participants for this project are native Malayalam speakers with clear articulation, consistent phonetic delivery, and proficiency in reading traditional formal Malayalam prose.

Because high acoustic consistency, identical mic calibration, and sustained recording discipline are essential across both the 50,000 isolated lexical items and the extended narrative chapters of Aithihyamala, this is structured primarily as a focused, individual-led project with a very small core team (at most 2 to 3 contributors) rather than an open crowd-sourced campaign.

17. What do you hope to learn from your work in this project or proposal? (required)

The project aims to answer the following Learning Questions:

1. Dual-Corpus Feasibility: How does recording sustained, continuous literary prose (Aithihyamala) impact production speed, vocal fatigue, and quality compared to rapid, isolated Lingua Libre recordings?
2. Audio Tooling &amp; Integration: What are the most efficient post-processing and batch-upload workflows for embedding accessible audio into Malayalam Wikisource alongside Wiktionary integration?
3. Community Accessibility &amp; Engagement: Does embedding native-speaker audio into Wikisource measurably enhance engagement and accessibility for visually impaired users and language learners?
4. Micro-Team Sustainability: What lightweight coordination practices keep a 2–3 person team efficient, acoustically consistent, and burnout-free throughout a high-volume dual recording project?
